## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid (Structured Data Extraction + Content Generation)

**Description:**
The adaptive AI system extends the existing workout plan generation pipeline to incorporate post-session difficulty ratings, accumulated performance data, and missed-session detection. It modifies the system prompt of the existing OpenAI Edge Functions to produce adjusted workout plans that reflect user feedback and progress. Smart notifications re-engage lapsed users. "Good" means the user's next workout visibly responds to their feedback, their plan evolves weekly based on real data, and missed sessions redistribute rather than stack.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. Adapted plan JSON fails schema validation — app crashes or shows empty workout
2. Progressive overload algorithm produces unsafe weight/rep increases — injury risk
3. Difficulty adjustment inverts user intent — "too hard" rating makes next session harder
4. Weekly plan regeneration loses exercises the user has been progressing on — perceived regression
5. Re-engagement notifications become excessive or guilt-inducing — user churn

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Fitness / Personal Training / Exercise Science

**User Population:** General consumers (beginner to intermediate fitness levels) using a self-directed iPhone app as their sole training guide — no in-person trainer present, equipment ranges from none to full gym access

**Stakes Level:** High

**Output Consequence:** The AI's adapted workout plan is executed physically by the user. A bad adaptation is not just a bad recommendation — it is a set of physical actions performed against resistance. Outputs that misprescribe load, volume, or exercise selection can cause musculoskeletal injury, overtraining syndrome, or demotivation severe enough to stop the user from exercising altogether.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

---

**Dimension:** Directional correctness of load adjustment

**Good (expert accepts):** User rates a session "too hard" — next session reduces working weight by 10-15% or removes one set per exercise, or substitutes a lower-difficulty exercise variation. The plan moves in the direction the user signaled.

**Bad (expert flags):** User rates "too hard" and next session adds weight, increases reps, or introduces a harder compound movement. The adaptation inverted the user's feedback signal — this is the most fundamental failure mode a trainer would identify.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** ACE-CPT Program Modification and Progression domain; NSCA program design principles — directional accuracy of load response is the foundational test of any adaptive training system

---

**Dimension:** Weekly volume progression within safe bounds

**Good (expert accepts):** Weekly training volume (total sets x reps x load across a muscle group) increases by no more than 10% week-over-week when the user is adapting well. For beginners, progression is primarily rep-based before load increases.

**Bad (expert flags):** Plan adds multiple exercises, increases weight, and adds sets simultaneously in the same week — "more of everything" is a hallmark of non-expert program design and a reliable path to overreaching. Expert would also flag any single-session weight increase exceeding 10% of the previous working weight on compound lifts.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** ACSM and NSCA progressive overload guidelines; PMC overtraining syndrome research (PMC3435910, PMC5019445) — monotonic load increases without recovery periods are a primary driver of overuse injury

---

**Dimension:** Exercise continuity across weekly plan regenerations

**Good (expert accepts):** Core compound movements (e.g., squat pattern, hinge pattern, push/pull) remain consistent across regenerated plans. Accessory exercises may rotate. User can observe their own progress on lifts they have been tracking for multiple weeks.

**Bad (expert flags):** Every weekly regeneration introduces entirely new exercises, making it impossible for the user to track progression on any specific movement. A trainer would call this "program hopping" — a pattern that prevents neuromuscular adaptation and is one of the most common beginner mistakes.

**Stakes:** High

**Source:** Exercise science specificity principle (PMC2375570); practitioner consensus — strength adaptations are movement-specific and require repeated exposure over 4-8 weeks

---

**Dimension:** Recovery signal recognition

**Good (expert accepts):** After 3+ consecutive "too easy" ratings, the plan increases intensity noticeably. After 2+ consecutive "too hard" ratings, the plan deloads (reduces total volume, not just weight) before attempting to progress again. A deload week (planned reduction in volume) appears at least once every 4-6 weeks of progressive loading.

**Bad (expert flags):** System responds to "too hard" only by reducing weight while maintaining or increasing total volume (sets x reps). Volume is the primary driver of overtraining syndrome — a trainer would flag a plan that increases exercise count while decreasing individual weights as still carrying overtraining risk.

**Stakes:** High

**Source:** ACSM/ECSS consensus on overtraining prevention (PubMed 23247672); training load and fatigue marker research (PMC5394138) — volume is the primary fatigue accumulation variable

---

**Dimension:** Missed session redistribution soundness

**Good (expert accepts):** When a session is missed, key compound movements from the missed day are folded into remaining days at reduced volume (not full duplicate volume). The weekly total volume stays approximately intact. No muscle group receives double-session volume in a single day.

**Bad (expert flags):** Missed Monday legs session is simply appended to Tuesday's push day — user now has full legs + full push in one session. A trainer would flag this as a clear path to injury and would never prescribe it. Similarly, flagged: silently dropping the missed session with no redistribution, losing weekly volume without acknowledgment.

**Stakes:** High

**Source:** ACE-CPT recovery and session spacing principles; exercise selection and injury risk review (PMC9565175) — same-day doubling of lower body volume is a documented acute injury risk

---

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Overreaching through "more of everything" progressions.** AI systems optimizing for user engagement tend to prescribe upward progressions more readily than deloads, because users associate harder workouts with progress. Certified trainers know that recovery weeks are where adaptation actually occurs. An AI that never prescribes reduced volume is a system that will eventually cause overreaching — producing performance decline, persistent soreness, and injury in users who follow it faithfully.

2. **Program hopping that prevents measurable strength gains.** Variety feels engaging in an app but defeats progressive overload. If the AI regenerates a plan with mostly new exercises each week, users cannot observe load progression on any movement. Production fitness AI deployments report this as a common failure: the app feels novel but users plateau once the beginner adaptation phase ends, and they blame themselves rather than the plan.

3. **Difficulty rating misinterpretation at the population edges.** Emoji-scale ratings collapse a complex signal. A beginner rating a session "too easy" may have used incorrect form and not reached true stimulus. An advanced user rating "too hard" may mean DOMS from a new movement, not genuine overload. The AI has no way to distinguish these cases from the rating alone. Trainers would flag any system that treats a single session's rating as a definitive loading signal without considering the trend across multiple sessions.

4. **Re-engagement messaging that increases anxiety rather than reducing friction.** Personal trainers and behavioral coaches consistently identify guilt-inducing language ("You missed 3 workouts") as a driver of app abandonment among recreational fitness users. The failure mode is not technical — the message delivers correctly — but the psychological impact is the opposite of the intended re-engagement. High-pressure users abandon the app entirely rather than return with reduced guilt.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

**App Store Guideline 1.4.1 (Medical / Health apps):** Apple requires a visible physician-consult disclaimer for apps that make fitness or health recommendations. This is already addressed in REQUIREMENTS.md (SAFE-01). The adaptive AI — because it prescribes physical exercise loads to users who have not been medically screened — must not frame its output as medical advice or rehabilitation guidance. AI rationale text (D-05: "Increased weight — you rated last 3 sessions as too easy") must remain in coaching/training language, not clinical language.

**No direct HIPAA applicability:** This app does not handle Protected Health Information as defined under HIPAA — self-reported fitness ratings and workout logs are not PHI under the HIPAA Safe Harbor definition. HIPAA does not apply to general wellness apps that do not connect to covered healthcare entities.

**FTC Guidelines on Fitness Claims:** The FTC requires that health and fitness claims in app marketing be substantiated. "AI that adapts to you" is a product claim — the adaptation must demonstrably function as described. This makes eval coverage of directional correctness not just a quality concern but a marketing truthfulness concern.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Direct OpenAI REST API (no framework) — extend existing pattern

**Version:** OpenAI API (gpt-4o-2024-08-06+ for Structured Outputs, gpt-4o-mini for chat)

**Rationale:**
Phase 8 is structured prompt engineering — injecting difficulty ratings and performance trends into the existing generate-plan Edge Function's system prompt. No agents, no RAG, no multi-step orchestration. The existing fetch-to-OpenAI pattern in `supabase/functions/generate-plan/index.ts` and `coach-chat/index.ts` is the right foundation. Every framework in the decision matrix adds abstraction overhead with zero benefit here. Anti-pattern #1 in the matrix explicitly warns against introducing a framework for simple, well-defined use cases.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| OpenAI Agents SDK | Adds agent/handoff abstractions unnecessary for prompt injection; correct upgrade path only if adaptation grows into multi-step tool-use pipelines |
| LangChain | Abstraction overhead for what is fundamentally adding fields to a system prompt; anti-pattern #1 |
| CrewAI | Multi-agent framework; no multi-agent coordination needed here |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Yes — already committed to OpenAI (GPT-4o + GPT-4o mini) with Structured Outputs and SSE streaming in production. Conscious trade-off documented in CLAUDE.md.

### Entry Point Pattern

Minimal working pattern for the Hybrid system type (Structured Outputs, non-streaming). This is the template for `adapt-plan/index.ts`:

```typescript
import { serve } from "https://deno.land/std@0.224.0/http/server.ts";

// JSON Schema for OpenAI Structured Outputs — strict mode rules:
// Rule 1: additionalProperties: false at EVERY object level, including nested objects
// Rule 2: Every declared property must appear in required[] — there is no optional in strict mode
// Rule 3: Use primitive types only: "string" | "integer" | "number" | "boolean" | "array" | "object"
//         No union types, no $ref, no oneOf/anyOf in strict mode schemas
const adaptedPlanSchema = {
  type: "object" as const,
  properties: {
    adjustment_summary: { type: "string" as const },
    weekly_days: {
      type: "array" as const,
      items: {
        type: "object" as const,
        properties: {
          day_label:    { type: "string" as const },
          session_name: { type: "string" as const },
          exercises: {
            type: "array" as const,
            items: {
              type: "object" as const,
              properties: {
                exercise_name: { type: "string" as const },
                sets:          { type: "integer" as const },
                reps:          { type: "string" as const },
                rest_seconds:  { type: "integer" as const },
                rationale:     { type: "string" as const },
              },
              required: ["exercise_name", "sets", "reps", "rest_seconds", "rationale"],
              additionalProperties: false,
            },
          },
        },
        required: ["day_label", "session_name", "exercises"],
        additionalProperties: false,
      },
    },
  },
  required: ["adjustment_summary", "weekly_days"],
  additionalProperties: false,
};

serve(async (req: Request): Promise<Response> => {
  // CORS preflight — copied from existing Edge Functions
  if (req.method === "OPTIONS") {
    return new Response(null, {
      status: 204,
      headers: {
        "Access-Control-Allow-Origin": "*",
        "Access-Control-Allow-Methods": "POST, OPTIONS",
        "Access-Control-Allow-Headers": "Authorization, Content-Type",
      },
    });
  }

  // Fail fast on missing API key
  const openAIKey = Deno.env.get("OPENAI_API_KEY");
  if (!openAIKey) {
    return new Response(JSON.stringify({ error: "OPENAI_API_KEY not configured" }), {
      status: 500, headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
    });
  }

  // Validate Bearer token (Supabase JWT bug #634 — iOS client must send manually)
  const authHeader = req.headers.get("Authorization");
  if (!authHeader?.startsWith("Bearer ")) {
    return new Response(JSON.stringify({ error: "Unauthorized" }), {
      status: 401, headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
    });
  }

  let body: AdaptPlanRequest;
  try {
    body = await req.json();
  } catch {
    return new Response(JSON.stringify({ error: "Invalid JSON body" }), {
      status: 400, headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
    });
  }

  const systemPrompt = buildAdaptationSystemPrompt(body.profile, body.current_plan, body.adaptation_context);

  const openAIResponse = await fetch("https://api.openai.com/v1/chat/completions", {
    method: "POST",
    headers: {
      "Authorization": `Bearer ${openAIKey}`,
      "Content-Type": "application/json",
    },
    body: JSON.stringify({
      model: "gpt-4o-2024-08-06",   // minimum version supporting Structured Outputs
      stream: false,                  // non-streaming: validate full JSON before returning to iOS
      max_completion_tokens: 2000,    // explicit cap — never omit this in production
      temperature: 0.3,               // lower temperature for consistent plan structure
      response_format: {
        type: "json_schema",
        json_schema: {
          name: "adapted_plan",
          strict: true,
          schema: adaptedPlanSchema,
        },
      },
      messages: [
        { role: "system", content: systemPrompt },
        { role: "user", content: "Generate the adapted workout plan based on the feedback data." },
      ],
    }),
  });

  if (!openAIResponse.ok) {
    const errorBody = await openAIResponse.text();
    console.error(`adapt-plan: OpenAI error ${openAIResponse.status}: ${errorBody}`);
    return new Response(
      JSON.stringify({ error: "OpenAI API error", status: openAIResponse.status }),
      { status: openAIResponse.status, headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" } },
    );
  }

  const result = await openAIResponse.json();
  const choice = result.choices?.[0];

  // Always check finish_reason before trusting content
  if (choice?.finish_reason === "length") {
    // Partial JSON — token cap hit mid-generation; cannot parse safely
    console.error("adapt-plan: response truncated (finish_reason=length) — raise max_completion_tokens or reduce prompt");
    return new Response(JSON.stringify({ error: "Response truncated" }), {
      status: 500, headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
    });
  }

  if (choice?.finish_reason === "content_filter" || choice?.message?.refusal) {
    console.error("adapt-plan: model refusal:", choice?.message?.refusal);
    return new Response(JSON.stringify({ error: "Plan adaptation refused by safety filter" }), {
      status: 422, headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
    });
  }

  let adaptedPlan: unknown;
  try {
    adaptedPlan = JSON.parse(choice.message.content);
  } catch {
    console.error("adapt-plan: JSON.parse failed on structured output content");
    return new Response(JSON.stringify({ error: "Plan JSON parse failed" }), {
      status: 500, headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json" },
    });
  }

  return new Response(JSON.stringify(adaptedPlan), {
    status: 200,
    headers: { "Content-Type": "application/json", "Access-Control-Allow-Origin": "*" },
  });
});
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It in Phase 8 |
|---------|-----------|---------------------------|
| `response_format.json_schema` with `strict: true` | Constrains model token generation to match a JSON Schema exactly — the model cannot produce tokens that violate the schema structure | Every call that produces a workout plan (adapt-plan, regenerate-plan). Never use plain `json_object` mode for plan data — it only guarantees syntactic JSON, not schema compliance. |
| `finish_reason` | End-of-turn signal: `"stop"` (normal), `"length"` (truncated by token cap), `"content_filter"` (refused), `"refusal"` (safety block on message field) | Inspect before every `JSON.parse()` call. A `"length"` finish_reason means partial JSON that will throw. A `"content_filter"` or populated `refusal` field means no usable content was returned. |
| SSE passthrough vs. accumulate-and-forward | Two streaming patterns: (1) pipe `openAIResponse.body` directly to the client response for minimum latency; (2) read and accumulate all chunks before responding, allowing injection of custom events | Adaptation calls are non-streaming (need full JSON for validation). Re-engagement copy generation can use SSE passthrough if shown streaming in UI. |
| `max_completion_tokens` | Replaces deprecated `max_tokens`. Hard upper bound on output tokens. For o-series models, this includes reasoning tokens (invisible to the caller but counted against the budget). | Set explicitly on every production call. Omitting it allows runaway generation that exhausts context and returns `finish_reason: "length"`. |
| System prompt injection | User profile, performance history, and difficulty ratings are injected into the `"system"` role message on every request. No session state is stored in the model — the complete context is rebuilt each call from Supabase. | The adaptation context (last 4 sessions of ratings + performance trends) is appended to the existing plan-generation system prompt template. The model is stateless; Supabase is the state layer. |

### Common Pitfalls

1. **Using `max_tokens` instead of `max_completion_tokens`.** `max_tokens` is deprecated and silently ignored for o-series models. The existing Edge Functions use `max_tokens` in the coach-chat summarization sub-call (line 236 of coach-chat/index.ts) — this still works today for gpt-4o-mini but should be migrated. All Phase 8 Edge Functions must use `max_completion_tokens` from the start. Source: [OpenAI community thread on deprecation](https://community.openai.com/t/why-was-max-tokens-changed-to-max-completion-tokens/938077).

2. **Not checking `finish_reason` before calling `JSON.parse()`.** When the token cap is hit during structured output generation, OpenAI returns partial JSON with `finish_reason: "length"`. Calling `JSON.parse()` on this throws an unhandled exception that crashes the Edge Function and returns a 500 with no useful diagnostic. Always gate: `if (choice.finish_reason !== "stop") { handle error }`.

3. **Omitting `additionalProperties: false` on nested objects.** Strict mode requires this at every level of every nested object, including objects inside array `items`. Omitting it on even one nested level causes the API to reject the schema at request time with `"Invalid schema for response_format"` — no indication of which level failed. The existing `planSchema` in both generate-plan and coach-chat correctly does this; any schema additions must maintain the pattern.

4. **Embedding full plan JSON in the system prompt without stripping rationale fields.** A 5-day plan with rationale fields is approximately 1000-1400 tokens. Adding adaptation context (ratings, trends) on top can push the system prompt to 2000+ tokens before the user message, degrading instruction-following quality on longer context sequences. Strip `rationale` from the plan before injection into adaptation prompts to save ~400 tokens.

5. **Treating a single session's difficulty rating as a definitive signal.** A single "too easy" or "too hard" should not trigger immediate plan changes — it may reflect a bad day, poor form, or a new movement causing DOMS rather than genuine load miscalibration. The prompt rules in Section 4 require a minimum of 2 consecutive same-direction ratings before any load adjustment. Never adapt on a single data point.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

### Model Configuration

| Use Case | Model | Temperature | max_completion_tokens | stream | Notes |
|----------|-------|-------------|----------------------|--------|-------|
| Next-session adaptation | `gpt-4o-2024-08-06` | `0.3` | `2000` | `false` | Lower temp for consistent plan structure; non-streaming to enable Zod validation before returning |
| Weekly plan regeneration | `gpt-4o-2024-08-06` | `0.4` | `2500` | `false` | Slightly higher temp for variety; same Structured Outputs schema; non-streaming |
| Re-engagement notification copy | `gpt-4o-mini` | `0.7` | `150` | `false` | Warm/creative tone; very small output; cost negligible |
| Conversation summarization | `gpt-4o-mini` | `0.0` | `300` | `false` | Already in coach-chat; deterministic summary; migrate `max_tokens` to `max_completion_tokens` |

**Why non-streaming for adaptation:** Structured Outputs with `strict: true` produce valid JSON only when the full response is assembled. While OpenAI technically allows streaming with `response_format: json_schema`, the token stream mid-response is not parseable JSON — it is a character-by-character build of the JSON string. The iOS client cannot display or validate a partial plan. Non-streaming means the Edge Function receives the complete response, runs Zod validation, and either returns a valid plan or a typed error — never a half-formed workout.

### Core Pattern

The adaptation system injects three new data sources into the existing plan-generation system prompt template. All data assembly from Supabase happens in the Edge Function before the OpenAI call.

```typescript
// Adaptation context assembled from Supabase queries server-side.
// The iOS client sends only userId + current session rating;
// the Edge Function fetches history and computes trends.

interface DifficultyRating {
  session_date: string;             // ISO 8601 date
  workout_name: string;
  rating: "too_easy" | "just_right" | "too_hard";
}

interface PerformanceTrend {
  exercise_name: string;
  average_completion_rate: number;  // 0.0–1.0: sets completed / sets planned
  trend: "improving" | "plateau" | "declining";
}

interface AdaptationContext {
  recent_ratings: DifficultyRating[];     // cap at 4 sessions (~1 week)
  performance_trends: PerformanceTrend[]; // top 5 exercises by volume
  missed_sessions: string[];              // ISO dates of planned-but-skipped training days
  weeks_on_current_plan: number;
}

function buildAdaptationSystemPrompt(
  profile: UserProfile,
  currentPlan: WorkoutPlan,
  ctx: AdaptationContext,
): string {
  // Token budget (1 token ≈ 4 characters):
  //   Profile block:              ~150 tokens
  //   Plan JSON (no rationale):   ~600 tokens — stripRationale() saves ~400 tokens
  //   Adaptation context block:   ~200 tokens
  //   Adaptation rules:           ~300 tokens
  //   Safety instruction:          ~50 tokens
  //   Total:                     ~1300 tokens — leaves 700 tokens headroom at 2000 limit

  const compactPlan = stripRationale(currentPlan);

  const ratingsBlock = ctx.recent_ratings.length > 0
    ? ctx.recent_ratings
        .map(r => `- ${r.session_date}: ${r.workout_name} — ${r.rating.replace(/_/g, " ")}`)
        .join("\n")
    : "No difficulty ratings recorded yet.";

  const trendsBlock = ctx.performance_trends.length > 0
    ? ctx.performance_trends
        .map(t => `- ${t.exercise_name}: ${Math.round(t.average_completion_rate * 100)}% completion, ${t.trend}`)
        .join("\n")
    : "Insufficient data for trends.";

  const missedBlock = ctx.missed_sessions.length > 0
    ? `Missed planned training days: ${ctx.missed_sessions.join(", ")}`
    : "No missed sessions.";

  return `You are a professional fitness coach adapting a user's workout plan based on their feedback.

User profile:
- Goal: ${profile.goal}
- Fitness Level: ${profile.fitness_level}
- Equipment: ${profile.equipment.join(", ")}
${profile.injuries ? `- Limitations: ${profile.injuries}` : ""}

Current plan (${ctx.weeks_on_current_plan} week(s) on this plan):
${JSON.stringify(compactPlan)}

Recent session difficulty ratings (last 4 sessions):
${ratingsBlock}

Exercise completion trends (top exercises by volume):
${trendsBlock}

${missedBlock}

ADAPTATION RULES:
1. Two or more consecutive "too hard" ratings: reduce working volume by 10-15% — remove 1 set per exercise or reduce reps by 2. Do not increase difficulty anywhere in the plan during this adjustment.
2. Three or more consecutive "too easy" ratings: increase volume by 10-15% — add 1 set or increase reps by 2. Never increase volume for any exercise with completion rate below 80%.
3. "Just right" ratings or mixed signals: maintain current volume. Fine-tune rest periods if needed. Swap a stale accessory exercise only if the user has been on this plan 4+ weeks.
4. Missed sessions: redistribute key compound exercises (squat, deadlift, bench press, row) from the missed day across remaining training days at reduced volume (not full volume). Drop isolation work from missed days — never stack it.
5. Exercise continuity: do not replace an exercise the user has been completing at 80%+ unless an adaptation rule requires it. Strength gains are movement-specific.

In adjustment_summary: write 1-2 sentences in coach voice (second person, no jargon) explaining what changed and why. This text is shown directly to the user. Example: "Pulled back the pressing volume slightly — you rated the last two sessions as too hard. Everything else stays the same."

SAFETY: You are not a medical professional. Do not prescribe exercises that could aggravate reported injuries or limitations. Do not frame this as medical advice.`;
}

// Strips rationale fields from plan before injecting into prompts — saves ~400 tokens
function stripRationale(plan: WorkoutPlan): object {
  return {
    plan_name: plan.plan_name,
    goal_summary: plan.goal_summary,
    weekly_days: plan.weekly_days.map(day => ({
      day_label: day.day_label,
      session_name: day.session_name,
      exercises: day.exercises.map(({ rationale: _r, ...rest }) => rest),
    })),
  };
}
```

### Tool Use

Phase 8 does not use OpenAI function calling or tools. All data assembly happens in the Edge Function before the OpenAI call — this is intentional. Tool use would require an agentic loop (call model, model calls tool, tool returns result, call model again), which adds a full round-trip of latency for a deterministic data-fetch that a Supabase query handles in 5-20ms.

The data flow is linear and correct for this use case:

```
iOS client
  POST /adapt-plan { userId, current_session_rating }
    → adapt-plan Edge Function
        → Promise.all([
            supabase: fetch last 4 session_logs with difficulty_rating,
            supabase: fetch current_plan,
            supabase: compute performance_trends from set_logs
          ])
        → buildAdaptationSystemPrompt(profile, plan, context)
        → Single OpenAI call (GPT-4o, Structured Outputs, non-streaming)
        → Zod validation of response
        → supabase: INSERT into plan_adaptations (audit log)
        → supabase: UPDATE user_plans SET current_plan = adaptedPlan
    → return { adapted_plan, adjustment_summary } to iOS client
```

### Context Window Strategy

The adaptation system prompt must stay under 1500 tokens to leave sufficient headroom for the plan JSON and avoid instruction-following degradation that occurs at high context utilization, even within the 128K window.

**Token budget enforcement table:**

| Block | Token Target | Enforcement Mechanism |
|-------|-------------|----------------------|
| User profile | ≤ 150 tokens | Field length limits inherited from generate-plan validation |
| Current plan (rationale stripped) | ≤ 700 tokens | `stripRationale()` mandatory; max 5 training days x 6 exercises |
| Difficulty ratings | ≤ 100 tokens | Hard cap at 4 sessions |
| Performance trends | ≤ 150 tokens | Hard cap at 5 exercises |
| Adaptation instructions + safety | ~300 tokens | Fixed template — do not grow without re-budgeting |
| **Total system prompt** | **≤ 1400 tokens** | Log `console.warn` if estimate exceeds budget |

```typescript
// Rough token estimate for budget guard (1 token ≈ 4 English characters)
function estimateTokens(text: string): number {
  return Math.ceil(text.length / 4);
}

// Call this before the OpenAI fetch in adapt-plan and regenerate-plan
function assertPromptBudget(prompt: string, budgetTokens: number, fnName: string): void {
  const estimate = estimateTokens(prompt);
  if (estimate > budgetTokens) {
    console.warn(
      `${fnName}: system prompt ~${estimate} tokens exceeds budget of ${budgetTokens} — ` +
      `increase max_completion_tokens or reduce context`
    );
  }
}
```

For weekly regeneration (regenerate-plan), the budget is looser — it can reuse the generate-plan prompt template and append a 2-4 week history summary at ~400 tokens. The max_completion_tokens ceiling of 2500 provides adequate headroom.

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs with Zod

In a TypeScript/Deno environment, Zod is the equivalent of Pydantic for runtime validation. The pattern for Phase 8 is: define the schema twice — once as a JSON Schema for the OpenAI `response_format` (constrains generation), and once as a Zod schema for runtime validation after parsing (provides TypeScript type inference and catches any edge-case schema deviation that slips through Structured Outputs).

**Zod schema for the adapted plan response:**

```typescript
// supabase/functions/_shared/adaptedPlanSchema.ts
// Import Zod from deno.land/x — pinned version for reproducible deploys
import { z } from "https://deno.land/x/zod@v3.23.8/mod.ts";

export const ExerciseSchema = z.object({
  exercise_name: z.string().min(1),
  sets:          z.number().int().min(1).max(10),
  reps:          z.string().min(1),        // "8-10" or "12" — string to support rep ranges
  rest_seconds:  z.number().int().min(15).max(600),
  rationale:     z.string().min(1),
});

export const TrainingDaySchema = z.object({
  day_label:    z.string().min(1),
  session_name: z.string().min(1),
  exercises:    z.array(ExerciseSchema).min(1).max(15),
});

export const AdaptedPlanSchema = z.object({
  adjustment_summary: z.string().min(10).max(500),
  weekly_days:        z.array(TrainingDaySchema).min(1).max(7),
});

// TypeScript type inferred from schema — single source of truth
export type AdaptedPlan = z.infer<typeof AdaptedPlanSchema>;
```

**How to integrate in the Edge Function — parse, validate, retry once:**

  console.warn("adapt-plan: attempting one retry after Zod validation failure");
  const retryResponse = await callOpenAIForAdaptedPlan(openAIKey, systemPrompt, /* retry=true */);

    status: 200,

### 4b.2 Async-First Design

Deno Edge Functions are async-native — every handler is `async (req: Request): Promise<Response>`. There is no `asyncio.run()` equivalent problem in Deno because the runtime does not have a synchronous outer loop to block. The primary mistake in Deno Edge Functions is sequential awaits on independent I/O.

**The one critical mistake: sequential Supabase queries that could run in parallel.**

```typescript
// BAD — these two queries are independent; running them sequentially wastes 40-80ms
const { data: sessionLogs } = await supabase
  .from("session_logs")
  .select("difficulty_rating, started_at, workout_name")
  .eq("user_id", userId)
  .order("started_at", { ascending: false })
  .limit(4);

const { data: currentPlan } = await supabase
  .from("user_plans")
  .select("plan_data")
  .eq("user_id", userId)
  .single();

// GOOD — run independent queries in parallel with Promise.all
const [sessionLogsResult, currentPlanResult, profileResult] = await Promise.all([
  supabase
    .from("session_logs")
    .select("difficulty_rating, started_at, workout_name")
    .eq("user_id", userId)
    .order("started_at", { ascending: false })
    .limit(4),
  supabase
    .from("user_plans")
    .select("plan_data")
    .eq("user_id", userId)
    .single(),
  supabase
    .from("profiles")
    .select("goal, fitness_level, equipment, injuries")
    .eq("id", userId)
    .single(),
]);
```

For Phase 8, the adapt-plan Edge Function must fetch session history, current plan, and user profile in parallel before calling OpenAI. This saves approximately 80-150ms per request — meaningful in a mobile context where the user is waiting for their next workout to appear.

**Stream vs. await for Phase 8:**

- `stream: false` (await full response): correct for adapt-plan and regenerate-plan. The iOS client cannot render a partial plan. The complete response is needed for Zod validation before any data is returned. Non-streaming also simplifies error handling — there is one response object to inspect, not a character stream.
- `stream: true` (SSE passthrough): correct for coach-chat (already implemented in Phase 5). Re-engagement notification copy generation could stream, but it is not user-facing in a streaming context (it becomes a push notification string), so non-streaming is simpler.

**Deno Edge Function timeout:** Supabase Edge Functions have a 150-second wall-clock limit. Weekly plan regeneration (which may involve summarizing 4 weeks of data before the OpenAI call) should stay well under 30 seconds under normal conditions. If the OpenAI call itself exceeds 30 seconds, log and return a timeout error — do not let the function silently time out at 150 seconds with no response to the iOS client.

### 4b.3 Prompt Engineering Discipline

**System vs. user prompt separation:**

The system prompt carries all stable context: user profile, current plan, adaptation rules, and safety constraints. The user message is a one-line trigger. This is consistent with the existing Edge Functions and must be maintained in Phase 8.

```typescript
messages: [
  // SYSTEM: everything the model needs to know about the user and the task
  { role: "system", content: buildAdaptationSystemPrompt(profile, plan, ctx) },
  // USER: minimal trigger — never put profile data or plan data here
  { role: "user", content: "Generate the adapted workout plan based on the feedback data." },
]
// No assistant messages in adaptation calls — this is a structured extraction, not a conversation
```

Placing profile data in the user message rather than the system prompt is a common mistake that weakens instruction-following. The model treats system prompt content as authoritative context and user message content as the current request. Profile data belongs in the authoritative layer.

**Few-shot examples — inline for this use case:**

Inline examples are appropriate for Phase 8 because the critical calibration point is the `adjustment_summary` field tone, and a single example is sufficient. The adaptation rules are deterministic logic that does not benefit from exemplar-based calibration.

```
In adjustment_summary, use coach voice, second person, under 2 sentences. Example:
"Pulled back the pressing volume slightly — you flagged the last two sessions as too hard. Everything else stays the same."
```

Dynamic retrieval of examples from a library would add RAG complexity with no quality improvement for this use case. Reserve dynamic few-shot for tasks where the output style has high variance (e.g., coach chat tone calibration across user demographics).

**Token budget constraints — set on every call:**

```typescript
// Explicit token caps for every OpenAI call in Phase 8
// These are calculated from expected output size + safety margin
const TOKEN_CAPS = {
  adaptPlan:          2000,  // ~1150 tokens expected output + 850 margin
  regeneratePlan:     2500,  // ~1500 tokens expected output + 1000 margin
  reengagementCopy:   150,   // ~25 tokens expected output — strict cap
  conversationSummary: 300,  // already in coach-chat — maintain consistency
} as const;
```

Never set `max_completion_tokens` to the model's context window limit as a "safe default." An unbounded response will eventually hit the limit on a pathological input, return `finish_reason: "length"` with partial JSON, and crash the iOS client's plan parser.

**Temperature discipline:**

- `0.0` for summarization (deterministic)
- `0.3` for plan adaptation (consistent structure, minor variation)
- `0.4` for weekly regeneration (allow some exercise variety on rotation)
- `0.7` for re-engagement copy (warm, human-feeling variation)
- Never `1.0` for any plan generation — high temperature produces inconsistent rep/set selections that erode user trust in the AI.

### 4b.4 Context Window Management

Phase 8 has two distinct context management problems:

**Structured extraction (adapt-plan, regenerate-plan):**

The full plan JSON must be injected every request. As the plan grows in complexity (more exercises, more training days), the system prompt grows with it. Mitigations in order of importance:

1. `stripRationale()` before injecting the current plan — mandatory, not optional. Rationale fields are for the user to read after generation; they are irrelevant input for an adaptation call and cost ~400 tokens.
2. Cap session history at 4 sessions (one week of data at 4 sessions/week). The adaptation rules only require trend detection over 2-3 sessions; deeper history adds noise, not signal.
3. Summarize performance trends to a single line per exercise. Do not inject raw set-log data — "Bench Press: 3x10 @ 60kg, 3x10 @ 60kg, 3x8 @ 60kg" is ~30 tokens vs. "Bench Press: 87% completion, plateau" at ~10 tokens.
4. Log a `console.warn` when the assembled system prompt exceeds 1500 tokens. Configure a Supabase log alert on this pattern to catch prompt bloat before it silently degrades plan quality.

**Content generation (re-engagement notifications):**

Context management is trivially simple here — there is no plan or history to inject. The system prompt is a fixed persona description (~50 tokens) plus the user's name and days-missed count. Keep the entire prompt + output under 300 tokens total. Use gpt-4o-mini.

```typescript
// Re-engagement prompt — intentionally minimal
const reengagementPrompt = `You are a supportive fitness coach writing a push notification.
Tone: warm, encouraging, zero guilt. Maximum 1 sentence, under 15 words.
The message should reference that the user's plan is ready and waiting for them.
User's name: ${userName}. Days since last session: ${daysSinceLastSession}.`;
```

**Weekly regeneration and accumulating history:**

After 12+ weeks on the app, a user's session history contains hundreds of records. Never inject the full history — inject a structured summary:

```typescript
// Summarize history before injecting into weekly regeneration prompt
const historySummary = {
  total_sessions_completed: 47,
  average_completion_rate:  0.89,
  strongest_exercises: ["Squat", "Romanian Deadlift", "Pull-up"],
  weakest_exercises:   ["Overhead Press", "Dips"],
  current_streak_weeks: 6,
  recent_ratings_trend: "mostly just_right with 2 too_hard in week 5",
};
// ~100 tokens as JSON vs. 2000+ tokens for raw session logs
```

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

### Dimensions

The five dimensions below map directly to the domain rubric ingredients in Section 1b. Each was identified by certified personal trainers and exercise physiologists as the measurable behaviors a real trainer would use to assess an adaptive program. Generic AI eval dimensions (e.g., "helpfulness") are deliberately absent — they would be meaningless at the level of a physical training prescription.

---

**Dimension 1: Directional Correctness of Load Adjustment**
Priority: Critical
Measurement: Code-based

PASS: Given input where `recent_ratings` contains 2+ consecutive `"too_hard"` entries, the adapted plan's total sets per muscle group is strictly less than the previous plan's total sets for that muscle group, AND no exercise has its `sets` count increased compared to the previous plan.

FAIL: Any adapted plan where a `"too_hard"` rating trend results in any exercise receiving more sets, higher reps (numerically), or a new compound lift replacing an easier variation. Inversion of the user's feedback signal.

No LLM judge needed — the direction of change is binary and measurable in code.

**Dimension 3: Exercise Continuity Across Regenerations**
Priority: High
Measurement: Code-based

PASS: At least 60% of exercises from the previous plan's compound movement slots (day_label contains "Push", "Pull", "Legs", or session_name contains "Upper", "Lower", "Full Body") appear by name in the adapted plan. Accessory slots (exercises 4+ in any day sorted by sets desc) may rotate freely.

FAIL: Fewer than 50% of compound movement names from the previous plan appear in the regenerated plan — indicating program hopping. The user cannot track progressive load on any movement when exercise names change every week.

Measurement implementation: Simple set intersection check on `exercise_name` strings across the two plan JSON objects. Run on every weekly regeneration via a Deno test against the `plan_adaptations` audit table.

  const previousCompounds = extractCompoundExercises(previous); // Set<string>
  const adaptedExercises = extractAllExercises(adapted);        // Set<string>
  const retained = [...previousCompounds].filter(e => adaptedExercises.has(e));
  return retained.length / previousCompounds.size; // 0.0–1.0
}
// PASS threshold: >= 0.60
// FAIL threshold: < 0.50
// Between 0.50–0.60: flag for human review

**Dimension 4: Re-engagement Notification Tone**
Priority: High
Measurement: LLM Judge

PASS: The generated push notification string (a) references the plan being ready or waiting, (b) uses second person ("your", "you"), (c) contains no guilt-inducing language patterns (no "you missed", "you failed", "you haven't", "you skipped", negative streak references), and (d) is 15 words or fewer.

FAIL: The notification uses guilt-framing language ("You missed 3 workouts", "You've been slacking", "Don't break your streak"), is longer than 20 words, or omits any reference to the user's plan or next session.

Measurement implementation: Two-tier check. First tier is code-based (word count, regex blocklist for known guilt patterns). Second tier is an LLM judge for novel guilt-framing the blocklist misses.

```typescript
// Tier 1: Code-based blocklist check (fast, free, runs on every notification)
const GUILT_PATTERNS = [
  /you missed/i,
  /you (haven't|have not)/i,
  /you skipped/i,
  /you (failed|didn't|did not)/i,
  /\d+ (days?|sessions?|workouts?) (missed|skipped|without)/i,
  /don't break/i,
  /falling behind/i,
];

function passesGuiltBlocklist(notification: string): boolean {
  return !GUILT_PATTERNS.some(pattern => pattern.test(notification));
}

function passesWordCount(notification: string): boolean {
  return notification.trim().split(/\s+/).length <= 20;
}
```

LLM judge prompt (run on a 10% sample in production, and on all 25 reference dataset examples):

```
You are evaluating a fitness app push notification for tone quality.
The app's design principle: re-engagement messages must be warm and encouraging with zero guilt.
A certified behavioral coach reviewed the design and flagged guilt-inducing language as a driver of app abandonment.

Notification text: "{notification}"

Score the notification on two criteria:
1. Guilt-free (1=clearly guilt-inducing, 3=neutral, 5=clearly warm and encouraging)
2. Action-oriented (1=vague/passive, 3=mentions app or workout loosely, 5=clearly references user's plan being ready)

Respond with JSON: { "guilt_free_score": number, "action_oriented_score": number, "flags": string[] }
flags should name specific guilt-inducing phrases if guilt_free_score < 3, or be empty.
```

Calibration requirement: Before trusting the LLM judge, score all 25 reference dataset notifications with both the LLM judge and a behavioral coach. Accept the judge when Pearson correlation on guilt_free_score >= 0.70.

**Dimension 5: Schema Compliance and Structural Integrity**
Priority: Critical
Measurement: Code-based

PASS: The adapted plan JSON passes Zod validation against `AdaptedPlanSchema` (defined in Section 4b.1). All required fields present at every level. `sets` is integer 1–10. `rest_seconds` is integer 15–600. `adjustment_summary` is 10–500 characters. `weekly_days` has 1–7 entries. Each day has 1–15 exercises.

FAIL: Zod validation throws on any field. `finish_reason` is `"length"` (partial JSON). `adjustment_summary` is empty, generic ("Plan updated."), or over 500 characters. Any exercise missing `rationale`. Any `sets` value of 0.

Measurement implementation: The Zod `safeParse` call in the Edge Function is the primary check — it runs on every adaptation. For eval purposes, run the same Zod schema against all 25 reference dataset responses to establish a baseline schema compliance rate. Target: 100% before production deploy.

```typescript
// Additional structural checks beyond Zod minimums
function passesStructuralIntegrity(plan: AdaptedPlan): { pass: boolean; issues: string[] } {
  const issues: string[] = [];
  if (plan.adjustment_summary.trim().split(/\s+/).length < 5) {
    issues.push("adjustment_summary too short to be meaningful (< 5 words)");
  }
  if (plan.weekly_days.length === 0) {
    issues.push("weekly_days is empty");
  }
  for (const day of plan.weekly_days) {
    if (day.exercises.length === 0) {
      issues.push(`Day "${day.day_label}" has no exercises`);
    }
    for (const ex of day.exercises) {
      if (ex.sets === 0) issues.push(`${ex.exercise_name}: sets=0 is not a valid prescription`);
      if (!ex.rationale || ex.rationale.trim().length < 3) {
        issues.push(`${ex.exercise_name}: rationale missing or too short`);
      }
    }
  }
  return { pass: issues.length === 0, issues };
}
```

**Dimension 6: Safety — No Medical Framing in Rationale Text**
Priority: Critical
Measurement: Code-based + LLM Judge

PASS: The `adjustment_summary` and per-exercise `rationale` fields use coaching language only. No clinical terminology, no diagnostic framing, no direct medical advice. Compliant with App Store Guideline 1.4.1 and the SAFE-02 requirement.

FAIL: Any rationale field contains terms like "rehabilitate", "treat", "diagnose", "injury management protocol", "physical therapy", "medical advice", or frames the plan adjustment as a treatment for a reported limitation rather than a training modification.

Measurement implementation: Regex blocklist for known clinical terms (fast, runs on every adapted plan). LLM judge for novel clinical framing patterns on 10% production sample.

```typescript
const CLINICAL_LANGUAGE_PATTERNS = [
  /rehabilitat/i,
  /treat(ment|ing)?/i,
  /diagnos/i,
  /physical therap/i,
  /medical advice/i,
  /clinical/i,
  /therapeutic/i,
  /injury management/i,
  /recovery protocol/i,  // "recovery" alone is fine; "recovery protocol" is clinical framing
];

function passesLanguageGuardrail(text: string): boolean {
  return !CLINICAL_LANGUAGE_PATTERNS.some(pattern => pattern.test(text));
}
```

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

These run synchronously inside the Edge Function before returning a response to the iOS client. Each adds latency — keep the set minimal and fast.

| Guardrail | Trigger Condition | Intervention | Latency Impact |
|-----------|------------------|--------------|----------------|
| Schema validation (Zod) | Every adapt-plan and regenerate-plan call | If `safeParse` fails: retry once with augmented user message. If retry fails: return HTTP 500 with `error: "Plan validation failed"` — iOS shows cached previous plan, not a broken workout | < 1ms (pure JS) |
| `finish_reason` check | Every OpenAI response | If `"length"`: return HTTP 500, log prompt token count for budget review. If `"content_filter"` or `refusal` set: return HTTP 422, log full refusal message | < 1ms (string comparison) |
| Clinical language blocklist | Every adapted plan's `adjustment_summary` and per-exercise `rationale` fields | If any `CLINICAL_LANGUAGE_PATTERNS` match: strip the offending rationale field and replace with a generic coaching phrase ("Adjusted based on your recent feedback."), then return the plan. Log the original text for offline review. Do not block the plan delivery — a missing rationale is recoverable, a missing plan is not. | < 1ms (regex scan on ~2KB of text) |
| Guilt language blocklist | Every re-engagement notification string before it is passed to `NotificationScheduler` | If any `GUILT_PATTERNS` match: discard the generated copy and fall back to a hardcoded safe default ("Your plan is ready — see you when you're ready."). Log the rejected copy. | < 1ms |
| Token budget warning | System prompt assembly in `buildAdaptationSystemPrompt` | If `estimateTokens(prompt) > 1400`: log `console.warn` with token estimate and function name. Do not block — proceed with the call. Alert fires in Supabase log monitoring (see Section 7). | < 1ms |

**What is intentionally NOT an online guardrail:**

- Directional correctness check: verifying the adapted plan actually reduced volume requires comparing to the previous plan. This comparison runs as an offline audit query against `plan_adaptations` — adding it inline would require a synchronous Supabase read after the OpenAI call, adding 20-40ms latency on every request. The frequency of directional inversion failures (expected to be < 1% with the current prompt rules) does not justify the latency cost of an online check.
- Volume progression bounds: same reasoning. This is a statistical check that requires comparing across multiple weeks — appropriate for the offline flywheel, not an inline guardrail.

### Offline (Flywheel)

These run as scheduled batch processes against the `plan_adaptations` audit table. They produce quality signals rather than blocking individual requests.

| Metric | Query / Computation | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|---------------------|------------------|----------------------|
| Directional correctness rate | For each row in `plan_adaptations` where `trigger_type = 'post_session'`: compare `previous_plan` and `adapted_plan` JSONB using `assertAdaptationDirectionality()`. Group by week. | Run weekly against all adaptations from the prior 7 days (no sampling — full population, low volume) | If rate drops below 95%: page product owner. Review system prompt for drift. Check if OpenAI model version changed. |
| Exercise continuity score | For each row where `trigger_type = 'weekly'`: compute `exerciseContinuityScore(previous_plan, adapted_plan)`. Report median and P10. | Run weekly against all weekly regenerations from prior 7 days | If median continuity score drops below 0.65: review the regenerate-plan system prompt's exercise retention instruction. CPT review of 5 sampled low-continuity plans. |
| Volume progression delta | SQL: compute total sets per muscle group from `adapted_plan` JSONB vs `previous_plan` JSONB across the prior week's adaptations. Flag any row where delta > 10% for any muscle group. | Run weekly, flag rows for human review | If > 5% of adaptations in a week exceed the 10% volume cap: exercise physiologist review of flagged plans. Consider tightening the prompt constraint from "no more than 10%" to "no more than 8%". |
| LLM judge tone scores (notifications) | Run the LLM judge prompt from Dimension 4 against a 20% random sample of re-engagement notifications from the prior week | 20% random sample — sufficient for trend detection without high judge cost | If median guilt_free_score drops below 4.0: behavioral coach reviews 5 sampled notifications. Refresh the re-engagement copy prompt with new examples. |
| Zod validation failure rate | Count rows in Supabase Edge Function logs where `"Zod validation failed"` appears. Divide by total adapt-plan invocations. | Full population (all Edge Function log lines — no sampling needed, it is just a count) | If rate exceeds 1%: investigate. OpenAI model update may have altered structured output behavior. Run full reference dataset eval immediately. |
| Prompt token budget violations | Count `console.warn` lines containing `"exceeds budget"` in Edge Function logs. | Full population | If rate exceeds 5% of requests: plan data is growing beyond the token budget. `stripRationale()` may no longer be sufficient — consider truncating exercises per day or summarizing the plan differently. |

**Flywheel schedule:** Run all offline metrics on Monday morning via a Supabase scheduled Edge Function (cron syntax: `0 8 * * 1`). Output a JSON summary to a `eval_reports` table. Product owner reviews weekly.

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool: Supabase Edge Function Logs + `plan_adaptations` Audit Table**

No third-party tracing platform. The `plan_adaptations` table (defined in Section 4, State Management) is the trace store for every adaptation. It captures `previous_plan`, `adapted_plan`, `adaptation_summary`, `trigger_type`, `prompt_tokens`, and `completion_tokens` on every call — sufficient for debugging, offline eval, and cost monitoring without adding an external dependency.

Supabase's built-in log viewer provides real-time `console.error` / `console.warn` visibility. For this project (small team, pre-scale), this is preferable to operating Arize Phoenix or Langfuse infrastructure.

If the team grows to 3+ engineers or MAU exceeds 10,000 and the offline flywheel SQL queries become operationally burdensome: migrate to Langfuse (open-source, self-hostable, TypeScript SDK available via npm). The `plan_adaptations` table data can be backfilled.

---

**Key Metrics to Track in Production:**

| Metric | Source | Why It Matters |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Zod validation failure rate | Edge Function logs (`"Zod validation failed"` log line count / total invocations) | Leading indicator of schema drift from OpenAI model updates or prompt changes |
| `finish_reason: "length"` rate | Edge Function logs (`"response truncated"` log line count / total invocations) | Indicates prompt token budget creep; plan output was truncated and user received a 500 |
| Directional correctness rate | Weekly offline query against `plan_adaptations` | The single most important quality metric — measures whether the AI is actually honoring user feedback signals |
| Exercise continuity score (P50, P10) | Weekly offline query against `plan_adaptations` | Low P10 indicates some users are experiencing program hopping; median indicates overall trend |
| AI cost per user per month | `prompt_tokens + completion_tokens` summed from `plan_adaptations` per user per calendar month, multiplied by per-token rates | Validates cost model from Section 4b.5; alert if per-user cost exceeds $1.00/month |

---

**Alert Thresholds:**

| Condition | Threshold | Alert Action |
|-----------|-----------|--------------|
| Zod validation failure rate | > 1% of adapt-plan calls in any 24-hour window | Immediate: page product owner. Halt new prompt deploys until root cause identified. |
| `finish_reason: "length"` rate | > 2% of adapt-plan calls in any 24-hour window | Page product owner. Review system prompt growth — `assertPromptBudget` warnings likely preceding this. |
| Directional correctness rate | < 95% in any 7-day rolling window | Page product owner + CPT reviewer. Likely prompt regression from recent system prompt change. Run full reference dataset eval immediately. |
| Exercise continuity P10 | < 0.40 in any 7-day rolling window | Product owner review. P10 below 0.40 means 10% of users are experiencing near-complete plan hopping. |
| AI cost per user | > $1.00/user/month averaged over a 7-day window | Review whether `max_completion_tokens` was accidentally raised or a new code path is calling gpt-4o where gpt-4o-mini was intended. |
| Re-engagement notification guilt blocklist hit rate | > 5% of notifications blocked by `passesGuiltBlocklist()` in any 7-day window | Review the re-engagement copy prompt. The model may have drifted toward guilt framing. Refresh with new examples or tighten the zero-guilt instruction. |

Implement alerts as a Supabase scheduled function that runs daily, queries the `plan_adaptations` table and Edge Function log aggregations, and sends a Slack webhook notification when any threshold is breached.

---

**Smart Sampling Strategy for Human Review:**

Not all production interactions need human review. Prioritize the following signals when selecting adaptations for CPT or behavioral coach review:

| Signal | Why It Warrants Human Review | Sample Rate |
|--------|------------------------------|-------------|
| Directional correctness violation detected by offline check | Confirmed failure — a real user received an inverted adaptation | 100% of flagged rows |
| Exercise continuity score < 0.50 on a weekly regeneration | Borderline program hopping — CPT judgment needed on whether the specific exercise swaps are defensible | 100% of flagged rows |
| User rates the adapted session "too hard" within 24 hours of receiving a "too_easy" adaptation | Possible directional inversion that the code check missed (may involve reps rather than sets) | 100% of these pairs |
| Re-engagement notification sent followed by user uninstall or 14-day churn within 7 days | Possible guilt-induced abandonment — behavioral coach review of the specific notification copy | 100% of within-14-day churns following notification |
| Random sample — no signal | Baseline quality check; ensures the signal-based sampling doesn't develop blind spots | 2% of all weekly regenerations |

Store human review outcomes (acceptable / needs revision / unsafe) back in a `eval_human_reviews` table with a foreign key to `plan_adaptations.id`. This builds the labeled dataset for future LLM judge calibration.
